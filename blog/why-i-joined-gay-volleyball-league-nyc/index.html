Why I Joined a Gay Volleyball League in NYC

May 1, 2024 • By Author •

If you live in New York City long enough, eventually a gay man will ask you if you play dodgeball, kickball, or volleyball. The LGBTQ+ sports league pipeline is a well-documented phenomenon. For me, it was Gotham Volleyball.

The Community Aspect

I joined Gotham initially because I wanted to get some exercise in the winter. What I actually found was an entire subculture. Gotham has been around since 1981, and it's a massive, multi-generational community of people who all share this one specific, ridiculous hobby.

The volleyball is fun, but the real appeal is the structure it provides to your social life. Instead of trying to coordinate drinks with six different friends who are all "so busy this week," you just see your team every week. You practice together, you play your match, and then you inevitably end up at a bar afterward dissecting exactly why you lost the second set.

The Safe Space

There's also something inherently valuable about participating in queer sports. For a lot of us, high school gym class wasn't exactly a supportive environment. Being able to learn a sport as an adult in a space where you don't have to perform masculinity or hide parts of yourself is incredibly freeing.

When our captain, Peter, drafts players for the Rebels in D11, he's explicitly looking for people with a good attitude. Nobody cares if you mess up a play. The GLOD (Gym Leader on Duty) will cheerfully reset the scoreboard while both teams congratulate each other on a good rally. It's competitive, but it's competitive with a very specific, joyful kind of energy that you only really find in LGBTQ+ leagues.
